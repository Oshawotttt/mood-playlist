## 0. How to read this
For each dataset:
- **Real or synthetic?** Was it collected from the real world, or generated?
- **Origin.** Where it comes from and how it was made.
- **Spotify API?** Did the values come from Spotify's API?
- **Recent songs?** Does it cover recent music, and can we extend it?
- **Audio?** Is there sound we can play or analyse, or only numbers and text?
- **What it is.** Raw data, human labels, code, or a trained model.

**Key pattern:**
- **Mainstream songs** (1, 2, 8, 11) come with **no audio**, only numbers and tags. Music4All (12) is the exception, with 30s clips.

## 1. Dataset by dataset

### 1. Spotify 1.2M+ Songs (Kaggle, rodolfofigueroa)
[Kaggle link](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/rodolfofigueroa/spotify-12m-songs)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. The uploader collected it in 2020 by calling Spotify's API with `spotipy`. The list of songs came from MusicBrainz, an open music catalogue.
- **What's inside:** one CSV, `tracks_features.csv`, about 346 MB with ~1.2M rows. Columns:
  - identity: Spotify track ID, name, album, artists, track/disc number, release date/year
  - Spotify audio features: danceability, energy, key, loudness, mode, speechiness, acousticness, instrumentalness, liveness, valence, tempo, time signature
- **Audio:** none. The numbers were computed by Spotify's own models, which are not public.
- **Recent songs:** no. It stops at 2020 and was never updated. Spotify switched off the audio-features endpoint for new apps in Nov 2024, so we can't regenerate it ourselves (but see §2).
- **What it is:** a data table only. No code or model.
- **Good for:** large-scale tabular EDA, clustering songs by mood, building a catalogue to recommend from.
- **Watch out:** the licence isn't stated. It has duplicates (the same song on several releases). It's skewed towards albums listed in MusicBrainz.

### 2. Spotify Tracks Dataset, 114k (Kaggle, maharshipandya)
[Kaggle link](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/maharshipandya/-spotify-tracks-dataset)
- **Real or synthetic:** real, collected from Spotify's API (uploaded Oct 2022). Built by sampling ~1,000 tracks for each of 114–125 genres.
- **What's inside:** one CSV, ~20 MB. Same audio features as #1, plus **popularity** (0–100) and **track_genre**.
- **Audio:** none.
- **Recent songs:** up to 2022 only.
- **What it is:** a data table only.
- **Good for:** the cleanest starting point for EDA. It's small, has genre labels, and has real Spotify IDs, so it can be joined with other sources.
- **Watch out:** the genre is the genre *the uploader searched for*, not a true label, so one song can appear under several genres. The genres are artificially balanced, so it doesn't reflect what people actually listen to.

### 3. MTG-Jamendo
[GitHub](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. Made by the Music Technology Group (UPF Barcelona) from music on **Jamendo**, a platform where independent artists release music under Creative Commons licences.
- **What's inside:**
  - **55,701 full-length tracks (MP3)**
  - 195 tags covering genre, instrument and **mood/theme**. The mood/theme subset has 18,486 tracks and 59 tags, such as *happy, relaxing, dark, energetic, film, party*.
  - Train/test splits, download scripts and baseline model code
- **Audio:** ✅ full tracks, legally downloadable.
- **Recent songs:** no. It's a fixed snapshot, and the songs are **not mainstream**: you won't find Taylor Swift.
- **What it is:** audio + labels + code (scripts and a baseline). The tags were added by the uploaders themselves, so they're **noisy**.
- **Good for:** training or testing a mood model on **real audio**.
- **Watch out:** the download is large (hundreds of GB for full tracks; there are smaller low-quality subsets). The mood tags are missing for most tracks.

### 4. DEAM (MediaEval Database for Emotional Analysis of Music)
[Website](https://cvml.unige.ch/databases/DEAM/)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. Built for the MediaEval 2013–2015 challenges from CC music (FMA, Jamendo, MedleyDB).
- **What's inside:**
  - **1,802** 45-second excerpts and full songs
  - **valence** (how positive) and **arousal** (how energetic), rated by humans for the whole song and **every second**
  - openSMILE audio features
- **Audio:** ✅ included (1.3 GB).
- **Recent songs:** no. Indie CC music.
- **What it is:** audio + **human emotion labels** + precomputed features.
- **Good for:** the most credible "ground truth" for mood. The per-second ratings show how mood changes inside a song.
- **Watch out:** small. The ratings are on a scale from 1 to 9.

### 5. PMEmo
[GitHub](https://github.com/HuiZhangDB/PMEmo)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. A 2018 study with 457 participants.
- **What's inside:**
  - **794 popular songs** (chart music, so more mainstream than DEAM)
  - static and per-0.5-second valence/arousal ratings
  - EDA signals (skin-response sensor readings, unrelated to our EDA notebook)
  - lyrics, user comments, precomputed features
- **Audio:** ⚠️ **chorus clips only**, because of copyright.
- **What it is:** audio clips + human emotion labels + features.
- **Good for:** mood labels on songs people actually know.

### 6. MusicCaps (Google)
[Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/googleai/musiccaps)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. Google paid musicians to describe **5,521** 10-second clips taken from AudioSet (YouTube videos).
- **What's inside:**
  - a CSV of **YouTube ID + start time + caption + aspect list**
  - example aspect list: "pop, mellow piano melody, high-pitched female vocal"
- **Audio:** ⚠️ **not included.** You download each clip from YouTube yourself, and some videos have been deleted.
- **What it is:** human text labels only.
- **Good for:** testing "prompt → music" matching. It's also the set Google used for MusicLM.

### 7. Song Describer Dataset
[GitHub](https://github.com/mulab-mir/song-describer-dataset) · [Zenodo audio](https://zenodo.org/records/10072001) · [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mulab-mir/song-describer)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. **Human-written** captions, collected in 2023.
- **What it is not:** a caption generator, or captions written by an AI.
- **What's inside:** **1,106 captions** for **706 tracks** taken from MTG-Jamendo, written by 142 volunteers (~22 words each). The repo code only loads and evaluates the data.
- **Audio:** ✅ included (MP3s on Zenodo).
- **What it is:** human text labels + audio. A **test set**, not a training set.
- **Good for:** checking whether "prompt → song" works. Each caption has a known matching song, so the test is whether we rank that song near the top.

### 8. Spotify Million Playlist Dataset (MPD)
[AIcrowd page](https://www.aicrowd.com/challenges/spotify-million-playlist-dataset-challenge)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. **1M playlists** made by US Spotify users, 2010–2017.
- **What's inside:** JSON with each playlist's **title** and **ordered list of Spotify track IDs**.
- **Audio:** none. Audio features: none (they'd have to be joined from #1 or #2).
- **Access:** ⚠️ no longer downloadable from AIcrowd. You have to request it from Spotify Research. Unofficial Kaggle copies exist, but their licence is unclear.
- **What it is:** raw data.
- **Good for:** the playlist titles ("chill", "breakup songs", "gym") act as free mood labels, and a title plus its tracks is a ready-made prompt–song pair for the learned-embedding extension.

### 9. FMA (Free Music Archive)
[GitHub](https://github.com/mdeff/fma)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. CC-licensed indie music from freemusicarchive.org.
- **What's inside:**
  - **106,574 tracks**, 161 genres
  - metadata (`tracks.csv`), librosa features, and old Echonest/Spotify-style features for 13k tracks
- **Audio:** ✅ in four sizes: 8k × 30s clips (7 GB), 25k × 30s (22 GB), 106k × 30s (93 GB), or full tracks (879 GB).
- **What it is:** audio + metadata + precomputed features + code examples.
- **Good for:** a large legal audio pool for extra audio-branch data and for playing in a demo.

### 10. Spotify Artist Streaming Analytics 2020–2025 (Kaggle) — synthetic
[Kaggle link](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/beamhonor0911/spotify-artist-streaming-analytics-20202025)
- **Real or synthetic:** ❌ **fully synthetic.** The uploader generated 50k fake tracks with code.
- **Audio, real IDs, real songs:** none.
- **Decision:** only for testing that code runs. No results from it are reported.
- **Why not use it at all:** any pattern in it (correlations, clusters) reflects how the generator was written, not real music. Mixing it with real data would contaminate the results. For the proposal, list it as "considered, rejected because it is synthetic" to show we checked data quality.

### 11. MuSe (Musical Sentiment Dataset)
[Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/cakiki/muse-the-musical-sentiment-dataset) · [Paper (CHR 2020)](http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2723/short26.pdf)
- **Real or synthetic:** real songs and real Last.fm tags. The emotion scores, however, are **computed**, not rated by people.
- **Origin:** Akiki & Burghardt (2021). They collected each song's Last.fm social tags, then scored the tags with the **Warriner et al. word lexicon** (a list of English words with human valence/arousal/dominance ratings).
- **What's inside (checked 2026-10-06, downloaded `muse_v3.csv`, 18 MB, 90,001 songs):**
  - columns: `lastfm_url`, `track`, `artist`, `seeds`, `number_of_emotion_tags`, `valence_tags`, `arousal_tags`, `dominance_tags`, `mbid`, `spotify_id`, `genre`
  - `seeds`: the mood word(s) the authors used to *find* the song on Last.fm. **276 distinct seeds** (aggressive, gloomy, lonely, calm, uplifting…), ~1,000 songs each, so the sample is artificially balanced across moods. **80% of songs have only one seed**
  - **valence, arousal, dominance**: lexicon scores of the song's emotion tags
  - missing: `spotify_id` 31.5%, `mbid` 32.0%, `genre` 7.4%; everything else complete
- **⚠️ No full tag list.** The CSV doesn't include each song's Last.fm tags, only the seeds and a *count* of emotion tags. No lyrics or descriptions either.
- **Audio:** none.
- **What it is:** a song list + seed mood words + lexicon scores.
- **Good for:**
  - a **mainstream song list** (90k songs with Last.fm URLs, 68% with Spotify IDs)
  - **weak mood labels**: the seeds are tags real Last.fm users applied
  - **Stage 0 vocabulary**: the 276 seed words are a ready-made list of music mood words
  - **not** enough text on its own for the text branch. Last.fm's `track.getTopTags` no longer returns tags for ~99% of songs (2026-10-09), so we fetch lyrics by MuSe's artist + track (LRCLIB), or use Music4All
- **Watch out:**
  - Valence/arousal are **word scores of the tags, not ratings of the music**. Checking our text branch against them is partly circular, since that branch also reads the tags.
  - The authors warn about **many songs sharing the same point** in sentiment space, because songs with few tags share one seed tag.
  - The authors call it a **proof of concept**.
  - Spotify ID coverage is partial. Measure it in EDA.

### 12. Music4All
[Request page](https://sites.google.com/view/contact4music4all) · Paper: Santana et al., IWSSIP 2020
- **Real or synthetic:** real.
- **What's inside (per the paper; not yet checked by us):**
  - **~109k songs**, each with a **30-second audio clip**
  - **lyrics**, Last.fm tags, genres, release year, popularity
  - Spotify-style features (energy, valence, danceability, tempo, key and so on)
  - listening histories of ~15k users
- **Audio:** ⚠️ 30s clips (mainstream music).
- **Access:** ⚠️ **request form, research use.** Not yet requested.
- **What it is:** audio + text + features for the same songs.
- **Good for:** the only source with **mainstream songs and both text and audio**. That makes it the best catalogue for training both branches and testing Fusion on one set of songs.
- **Watch out:** 30s clips may miss parts of a song. Lyrics licensing limits redistribution, so don't republish them. Check the licence terms once access is granted.

### 13. GoEmotions
[GitHub](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/goemotions) · [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google-research-datasets/go_emotions) · [Paper (ACL 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00547)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. Reddit comments labelled by human raters (several per comment).
- **What's inside:** **~58k English Reddit comments**, each labelled with one or more of **27 emotions + neutral**.
- **Audio:** n/a (text only).
- **What it is:** human emotion labels on everyday text.
- **Good for:** training and testing the Prompt Encoder (Stage 2). It's the closest public data to "how people describe feelings". Its emotion list also informs Stage 0.
- **Watch out:**
  - Reddit comments are not music requests: different length, topics and tone.
  - Labels are **imbalanced** (neutral and admiration are common; grief, pride, relief are rare).
  - An external audit reported a large share of **mislabelled** examples.
  - Mapping 27 emotions onto our dimensions loses information.

### 14. EmpatheticDialogues
[GitHub (archived)](https://github.com/facebookresearch/EmpatheticDialogues) · [HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/empathetic_dialogues) · [Paper (ACL 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00207) · [Direct download (28 MB)](https://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/parlai/empatheticdialogues/empatheticdialogues.tar.gz)
- **Real or synthetic:** real. Written by crowdworkers. Per the paper, each writer picked an emotion, described a situation from their own life where they felt it, then had a short conversation about it with another worker.
- **Checked by us (2026-10-06, downloaded the files):**
  - three CSVs (train / valid / test): **24,850 conversations**, 107k utterances. Each conversation has one situation, so **24,850 situations** (24,503 unique)
  - columns: `conv_id`, `utterance_idx`, `context` (the emotion label), `prompt` (the situation text), `speaker_idx`, `utterance`, `selfeval`, `tags`
  - **32 emotion labels, one per situation, fairly balanced**: from 1,279 (surprised) down to 478 (faithful); most labels have 700–900
  - situations are short: **median 16 words** (10th–90th percentile 8–32), and 76% start with "I", "my", "we" and so on
  - examples: *"My dog died and I was just heartbroken…"* (devastated), *"I love looking at old photos. Makes me miss the good old days."* (sentimental)
- **Audio:** n/a (text only).
- **What it is:** first-person emotional situations, each with one emotion label chosen by its writer.
- **Good for:**
  - **training and testing the Prompt Encoder (Stage 2).** The `prompt` field is the closest public text we've found to what users will type ("I'm going through a breakup"): short, first person, about a life event. It complements GoEmotions, whose Reddit comments are about anything
  - **Stage 0:** several labels map directly onto music emotions: sad, devastated, lonely, nostalgic, sentimental, hopeful, anxious, joyful, content, excited, afraid
  - **Stage 3:** a pool of realistic situations to draw the test prompts from, instead of writing them all ourselves
- **Watch out:**
  - **One label per situation**, but our Prompt Encoder outputs scores for every dimension. "Breakup" is sad *and* lonely *and* maybe nostalgic, and the data only says one of them. Treat a label as "this one is high", not "the others are zero".
  - **The writer chose the label**, with no second rater, so there's no agreement score. Some pairs look noisy (*"My brother was working in my office"* → sentimental).
  - **Several labels aren't music emotions:** guilty, ashamed, jealous, embarrassed, disgusted, impressed, prepared, faithful, trusting. They need mapping, or we drop them.
  - Situations are about life events, not music (only ~130 mention "music" or "song"). That's fine for prompts, but no use on the song side.
  - **File format:** commas are written as `_comma_`. In valid and test, about half the rows have a 9th column (retrieval candidates for the original benchmark), so a plain `pd.read_csv` fails. Read the first 8 fields only, or use the HuggingFace copy.
  - **Licence: CC BY-NC 4.0** (non-commercial). Fine for a student project, but it can't go into a commercial product. The GitHub repo was archived in Oct 2023; the download still works.

- **Since Nov 2024:** new apps can't get audio features or recommendations.
- **Since Feb 2026, Development Mode apps:**
  - the **app owner needs Premium**
  - there is a limit of **1 app per developer** and **5 users per app**
  - some endpoint families were removed
  - search returns at most 10 results

| Source | Tested? | What you get | Audio? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Answer:** yes, we can get recent songs.
- **ReccoBeats** gives the same numbers as datasets #1 and #2. We use it only for the valence × energy baseline and the EDA Q7 check, not for training.
- **Deezer previews** give 30 seconds of real audio for analysis.
- We can't get **full-length** mainstream audio legally.

## 3. MTG-Jamendo labels, training a model for Spotify songs, and getting audio

### Who labelled MTG-Jamendo?
- **The uploaders** (artists or labels on Jamendo) tagged their own tracks when they uploaded them. No paid annotators were used, and no code assigned the tags.
- MTG then **cleaned** the tags: merged synonyms (`tag_map.json`), dropped tracks under 30s, and kept only tags used by ≥ 50 different artists. They didn't re-label anything.
- **Exception:** the [Music Classification Annotations](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset/blob/master/derived/music-classification-annotations/README.md) subset of the test split was **re-labelled by human annotators** (mostly three per track). Only tracks where all annotators agreed were kept (411–8,756 tracks depending on the label type). That makes it a cleaner test set.
- **What this means:** the labels are **noisy and incomplete**. Artists tag selectively, a missing tag doesn't mean "not this mood", and artists may pick tags for marketing.

### Can it train a model to classify Spotify songs?
**Yes, as long as the model works on audio.** It can't learn from Spotify's numbers, because Jamendo has none.
- **Train:** audio (or audio embeddings) → mood tags, on MTG-Jamendo.
- **Apply:** run the model on **30-second previews** of Spotify songs (from Deezer or iTunes; see below).
- **Pretrained shortcut:** [Essentia](https://essentia.upf.edu/models.html) already provides models trained on MTG-Jamendo, for mood/theme, genre and instrument. We can use them directly or fine-tune them, so we don't have to train from scratch.
- **Risks:**
  1. **Domain shift.** The model learns from indie CC music, which sounds different from mainstream production. Check it on a hand-labelled sample of Spotify songs.
  2. **Noisy labels** (see above).
  3. **Imbalanced tags.** A few moods dominate.
  4. **Previews are 30 seconds.** The clip may miss the part of the song that carries its mood (e.g. a quiet intro or a big final chorus).
- **Alternative without audio:** use Spotify-style numbers (valence, energy and so on, from ReccoBeats or Kaggle) and define mood as regions of valence × energy. Simpler, but we can't control or check the underlying model.

**Best approach:**
- **Analysis and classification:** 30s previews from iTunes or Deezer.
- **Playing the results in the demo:** Spotify playback control for mainstream songs (Web API / Web Playback SDK; owner needs Premium, ≤5 users), or CC audio played directly.

### 5.2 Tag vocabulary (Stage 0: picking the dimensions)
- Collect the 276 MuSe seed words, plus full tags from the Last.fm API and Music4All if we get them (MTG-Jamendo's 59 mood tags are added in Phase 2). Count frequencies and plot the long tail.
- Drop non-emotion tags ("seen live", "favourites", genre names, artist names).
- Group synonyms (sad / melancholic / depressing → sadness). Show the mapping table.
- Map the remaining groups onto GEMS, valence/arousal and GoEmotions. List the **themes and situations** that don't fit (heartbreak, party, late night, workout) as candidate extra dimensions.
- Count how many songs each candidate dimension would cover. A dimension with very few songs can't be trained or tested.

### 5.3 Label quality
- **MTG-Jamendo (Phase 2):** mood tag frequency, tags per track, co-occurrence. Compare uploader tags with the human re-labelled test subset (how often do they agree?).
- **PMEmo (Phase 1) / DEAM (Phase 2):** distribution of valence and arousal. Spread between annotators (standard deviation per song) as a measure of how subjective each song is.
- **GoEmotions:** label frequency, % multi-label, share of "neutral", rater agreement per emotion.
- **MuSe:** how many songs rely on a single seed tag. Size of the shared-point duplicate problem.
- **Jev (once labelling starts):** agreement with the human labels above, per dimension.

### 5.4 Text
- Length distributions (tokens) of: tags per song, Music4All lyrics, Song Describer captions, GoEmotions comments, and our own test prompts. How far is the training text from what users will type?
- Top words / n-grams per source, and per emotion. Word clouds for the proposal.
- **Vocabulary overlap** between prompt-side text (GoEmotions, test prompts) and song-side text (tags, captions). Low overlap means the two encoders see very different language.

### 5.5 Audio (Phase 2; the template asks for audio checks)
On a sample of MTG-Jamendo, DEAM, Music4All clips and Deezer/iTunes previews:
- sample rate, channels, duration distribution
- RMS loudness, zero-crossing rate, spectral centroid
- silence ratio, an estimate of signal-to-noise, clipping
- **Domain-shift check:** compare these distributions between indie CC audio (MTG-Jamendo, DEAM) and mainstream audio (Music4All, previews). Big differences warn that the audio branch may not transfer.
- Example mel-spectrograms per mood, for the proposal.
